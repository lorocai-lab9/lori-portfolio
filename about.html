About Lori

A designer who works where ambiguity lives.

I work best in complex, constraint-heavy environments — early-stage products and system redesigns where the real challenge is uncertainty, cross-functional trade-offs, and shaping something scalable from a blurry start.

Approach

Why product, design, and research all matter.

Working across these three disciplines reshaped how I make design decisions — leaning on problem framing and evidence over taste alone.

  • Product mindset keeps the work tied to outcomes — not just artifacts.
  • Research keeps the work honest — assumptions get tested before they harden into features.
  • Design holds it all together — translating constraints into something people actually want to use.

Principles

What I care about when design gets hard.

01

Clarifying the Core

Prioritize the few decisions that actually move the product forward — and let the rest follow.

02

Embracing Constraints

Focus on the high-frequency actions. The 80% the user does every day matters more than the 20% they might do once.

03

Intentional Partnership

Use prototypes — not opinions — to align stakeholders. Show the trade-off, then decide.

04

System-Level Thinking

Favor scalable patterns over one-off fixes. The interaction you design today is the foundation for tomorrow's feature.

05

Iteration as Risk Reduction

Test the riskiest assumption first. Cheap iterations early prevent expensive ones later.

06

Designing for Transitions

State changes are where users get lost. Pay attention to the seams between flows, not just the screens.

Trajectory

How my thinking has evolved.

Advertising → business development → HCI → UX design. Each stop reframed the previous one.

  • From advertising: how stories carry meaning, and why narrative shapes adoption.
  • From business development: how to align user value with the metrics that keep a product alive.
  • From HCI: how research turns intuition into evidence — and how systems behave at scale.
  • Where it points now: making intelligent systems more human-centered.
